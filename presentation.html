GdzieZamieszkać means "where to live". Frame it as a district decision tool and a public service, not another property search site.

The problem

A home is a unit. A life happens in a district.

Decision support, not listings
Today

People compare flats

Price, rooms, photos and an address are easy to find. The day-to-day trade-offs around that address sit in scattered maps, registers and reports from different institutions, in different formats and at different dates.

Our answer

Compare places to live

All 18 districts of Kraków side by side: transport, amenities, air, noise, green space, road safety and housing costs. Every number shows where it comes from, when, and how reliable it is.

It is not a listings site. It shows data about districts, never offers. It never fills a gap with invented data, and it never calls a district the best for everyone.

Add one real statistic here with its source, for example how many people move to or within Kraków each year. Do not say a number aloud that is not on a slide with a source.

Who it is for

People choosing where to live, not data experts.

Couples and families

Schools, nurseries, parks and family friendliness, weighed against price.

Students and newcomers

Transport at night, commute times and asking rents, in Polish or English.

Seniors

Clinics, walkability, quiet streets and distance to emergency services.

Remote workers and buyers

Green space and noise, sale price history and a rent-versus-buy estimate.

Designed for a phone first, 320 pixels wide, and for screen readers, keyboards and zoomed screens. Target: WCAG 2.2 level AA. Polish by default, English as a toggle.
Five persona presets exist in the backend today: student, family, remote worker, senior and budget.

Score and report

The code scores. The model tells the story.

Reports pre-generated, never live
Metrics

Real rows

One district's values, with source, date and data kind for each.

Score

Calculated in code

Percentile ranks within Kraków, 0 to 100, each category counting equally, or your own weights. Tested.

Narrate

A short report

A language model writes 100–140 words from the facts only. A guard rejects any number not in the facts.

Store

Polish and English

36 reports, each written natively in its language and stored. Labelled as AI-written in the app.

AI-written report · Stare Miasto, stored excerpt

“Stare Miasto scores 40.9 out of 100 on our livability scale, placing it 16th among Kraków's 18 districts. Its strongest points come from amenities and transit: it ranks 1st for schools with 58 facilities (10.4 per km²) […] and 1st for night public transport with 910 departures per night. On the downside, it records 0.97 road casualties involving pedestrians or cyclists per km per year, ranking last (18th) […] Recorded-crime data is not included for this city.”

A score compares the districts of one city only. With no weights, the ranking equals the stored default score; a test checks that.

For the City of Kraków

A public service the city can host.

Ready to host

A free portal with no login, built on the city's own open data and national registers. The backend ships as a Docker image the city can run on its own servers.

Privacy and low risk

Read-only. Stores nothing about its users: no accounts, cookies or analytics, and no third-party requests. Every figure is sourced, so the city can stand behind it.

Accessible from the start

Built to WCAG 2.2 level AA, as Polish law requires of public bodies, with a draft accessibility statement for the city to complete.

A map of data gaps

Each missing metric says why. That tells the city which datasets to publish next, such as district crime figures or a population count.

Planned: official notices

Planned projects and infrastructure, published by the city, dated and sourced. Never presented as a price forecast.

Planned: residents' voice

Rent reports and data corrections with identity checked through the national login (Profil Zaufany), and anonymous counts of what people look for, for housing and transport planning.

The last two cards are planned work: each needs an API change and a new service first. They are not in the demo.

Lead with the three things that exist: hostable, private, accessible. Be clear that notices and resident feedback are the next phase.

Architecture

Public sources in. One audited, read-only service out.

Sources

Public data only

GTFS, OpenStreetMap, GIOŚ, Copernicus, the city noise map, BIP, RCN, police road data, a rent snapshot.

Database

Audited store

Postgres with PostGIS. Provenance on every row, row-level security, a read-only role for the API.

API

FastAPI, read-only

16 operations under an OpenAPI 3.1 contract, 153 offline tests. Serves a snapshot from memory.

App

React, PL and EN

Types generated from the contract. Accessible map, tables and charts. No third-party calls.

Security. Rate limits, strict security headers and content policy, CORS limited to the city's frontend, no secrets in the repository.
Quality. District areas match official areas within 0.2%. The audit caught real defects, such as an invalid air index value and future-dated deeds, before launch.
Nothing is translated or computed at request time except the personal ranking, which uses the same scoring code as the stored score.

Disclosure

How we use artificial intelligence.

Not AI

The score

Computed in code from percentile ranks, and tested. A model never computes or changes it.

District reports

z-ai/glm-5.3-flash

Through OpenRouter. Writes each report from a list of facts. Generated ahead of time, checked, stored.

Translation

deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash

Through OpenRouter. Translates stored notes, after a glossary and with a number check. Never at request time.

Development

Claude Code

Helped write and review code and documents. The team reviewed all of it and can explain it.

The event rules require disclosure of significant AI use. The app labels every report as AI-written.

Roadmap

Ship a trustworthy slice, then grow with the city.

Phase 1 · HackYeah

Demo slice

  • Districts list and map
  • District detail with price history, commute and rent versus buy
  • Compare, find a district, sources, accessibility statement
Phase 2

Household profile

  • Household type, children's ages, work place, budget
  • Turned into weights and filters in the browser; never stored
  • Commute from your work district next to each result
Phase 3 · with the city

Public-service features

  • Official notices of planned projects
  • Resident feedback with verified identity
  • Anonymous demand counts for planning
Beyond Kraków. One codebase, one deployment per city. A second city is a new database schema, not new code: the same engine already runs on Warsaw data in our private build.
Phase 3 needs a contract change and a backend service for each feature. We do not build screens against imaginary endpoints.
